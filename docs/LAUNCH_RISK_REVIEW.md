## Legal and organizer work that affects launch

### RISK-01 — Payable price and buyer-paid processing

The FTC's live-event ticket rule requires prominent upfront totals including unavoidable calculable fees for covered events. Applicability to this particular fundraiser needs assessment; implement transparent all-in pricing regardless. A later “fees at checkout” disclosure does not meet that standard for covered offers. [FTC fee-rule FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-frequently-asked-questions).

Visa defines a surcharge by its substance, restricts it to credit cards, caps it at the lower of the applicable merchant discount rate or 3%, and requires acquirer notice at least 30 days beforehand plus transaction/disclosure requirements. Debit/prepaid cards cannot be surcharged. There are only 16 days from this review to November 19; a new notice today would not satisfy that lead time. [Visa merchant surcharge requirements](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/global/support-legal/documents/merchant-surcharging-qa-for-web.pdf). Texas's credit-card surcharge law has an enforcement/court-history complication, while debit restrictions also matter; do not rely on the statute text alone or declare all fees lawful. [Texas State Law Library](https://www.sll.texas.gov/faqs/credit-card-surcharge/).

For this site, $0.61 above a $10 base is 6.1% of that base. The existing gross-up calculation therefore cannot automatically be approved as a credit-card surcharge. A separate line item or renamed “service fee” is not proof of compliance.

Planned resolution: preserve the goal that buyers cover the economics, and evaluate a genuine all-in ticket price applied consistently across payment methods. Have the merchant/processor resolve the treatment of the existing fee-inclusive prices before activation; do not change the price or represent this as a guaranteed legal workaround. Reuse existing approved Stripe prices where compatible. No double gross-up. Record the chosen policy once, reflect it in ads/site/cart/Stripe/receipts, and keep chapter and brother calculations tied to the approved base amounts. If a lawful surcharge mechanism is instead selected, validate supported card types, processor fields, notifications and timing before enabling it. Keep the fee-policy gate closed until resolved.

### RISK-02 — Seller, charity and tax facts

Publish the actual responsible organizer/seller and support contact (`zachzapalac@gmail.com`), not an invented corporation, charity or university endorsement. Match the receipt/statement descriptor to a recognizable identity and explain the beneficiary relationship accurately. Receiving funds in a person's bank account does not itself establish a charitable deduction or determine who legally operates the event.

Do not claim tickets are tax-deductible donations. IRS guidance excludes gifts to individuals and limits eligible quid-pro-quo deductions to amounts above benefits received. [IRS charitable contributions](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc506). Record the recipient and remittance process, allowed expenses and final reconciliation; do not advertise “100% to Movember” unless that is demonstrably true.

Establish sales-tax treatment for food/admission and any applicable student-organization exemption using the actual seller, certification and year-to-date sales. Texas has specific qualified student-organization provisions; a small event or charitable purpose alone is not proof of eligibility. If tax is owed, plan collection/remittance and update totals, imports and refund tests before sales; current import rejection of unexpected tax is not tax compliance. [Texas Comptroller nonprofit and student-organization guidance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-122.php).

### RISK-03 — Purchase terms buyers can actually use

Add concise purchase terms linked next to the purchase action and in the footer/receipt: event/date/location, ticket inclusions, food availability, transfers/replacements, ordinary refund policy, cancellation/postponement/severe-weather remedy, refund request channel, timeframes and treatment of processing fees. Preserve any mandatory consumer rights. The existing “ordinary purchases nonrefundable” rule can remain subject to those exceptions; do not invent a cancellation policy on behalf of the organizer.

Capture the accepted terms version and timestamp with the order before payment. Keep an archived version for support/disputes. Any retained direct Payment Link must present equivalent disclosures and acceptance evidence; otherwise remove it from the active sales journey while retaining historical fulfillment. Keep optional marketing or photo permission separate from ticket terms. Do not add blanket releases, forced arbitration or jurisdiction clauses as unreviewed boilerplate.

### RISK-04 — Food, premises and university approvals

Arlington requires a temporary establishment permit for public open/perishable-food service, with applications at least 10 days before the first event to avoid late fees. Documented nonprofit exceptions still require inspections and food-safety compliance; the city also identifies an allergen-poster requirement. Obtain the applicable determination and inspection/permit evidence for the actual activity rather than assuming fundraising is exempt. [Arlington food inspection program](https://www.arlingtontx.gov/Business/Health-Services/Health-Services/Retail-Temporary-Food-Inspection-Program).

UTA lists 5 class days for fundraising/space approvals and 14 class days for serving/cooking food on campus and outside-vendor food waivers. Campus activities on November 17–18 make this time-sensitive; verify existing approvals now. A submission is not a confirmed space reservation. [UTA event registration](https://www.uta.edu/student-affairs/student-organizations/officer-resources/planning-and-registration).

Organizer evidence: venue owner's permission, applicable lawful occupancy/egress requirements, accessible arrival information, food handling arrangements and actual ingredient/allergen labels. Keep “no sales cap” as the user's preference, but do not publish “unlimited capacity” or override a venue/authority limit. If a lawful limit is identified, implement a concurrent inventory cap before selling against it. Do not claim allergy-free preparation or an accessible venue without evidence. Pending steak details may stay disabled.

### RISK-05 — Activity safety and coverage

Have the chapter/adviser and insurer assess the actual pie/slap, hanging, volleyball and timed-eating activities, participation eligibility and any required consent. Confirm optional participation, incident response and authority to stop an activity. Slapping and timed eating introduce injury risks that a website waiver cannot remove; omit or replace any activity the responsible reviewers cannot approve. Do not silently change packet scoring during this review. UTA requires current risk-management training for registered organizations. [UTA risk management](https://www.uta.edu/student-affairs/student-organizations/officer-resources/risk-management).

Record actual coverage and exclusions; do not assume a national fraternity policy or homeowner policy covers this event. Avoid inventing alcohol service, raffles or age restrictions not in the scope; re-review if those are added. No website statement should imply that consent makes a prohibited activity permissible.

### RISK-06 — Privacy, retention and email

Publish a truthful notice describing names/emails, ticket and payment references, chapter/seller attribution, staff activity and technical logs; purposes; actual processors; support/deletion requests; security limitations; and retention. Inventory Stripe, hosting, Neon, Brevo and the scheduler, distinguishing buyer data from a scheduler's job URL/authentication only. Texas's privacy law generally exempts SBA-defined small businesses except specified sensitive-data sales; determine actual applicability rather than adding an indiscriminate GDPR/CCPA checklist. [Texas AG privacy guidance](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/consumer-privacy-rights/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act).

Engineering proposal, pending financial/legal retention needs: remove abandoned unneeded contact details after 30 days; set operational logs to 30 days where supported; expire event-only staff sessions/access after operations close; review attendee-identifying data 90 days after the event for deletion/anonymization. Retain only required financial/dispute evidence under a documented schedule/hold, with backup expiration and processor limits disclosed. These are proposed product defaults, not statutory deadlines. Build a dry-run deletion process and prevent retained backups from silently reintroducing deleted data after restore. Do not promise immediate deletion everywhere or irreversible deletion while recoverable copies remain.

Keep brother donations and buyer details private; public standings contain aggregate chapter scores only. Limit what scanner roles see. Review logs, analytics, error reports, URLs and CSV exports for sensitive data and ticket bearer tokens. Use aggregate diagnostics and no advertising trackers by default; add a consent mechanism only if actual tools/jurisdictions require it, not a decorative cookie banner.

Ticket emails remain transactional: truthful sender/reply-to, no unrelated promotional campaign or silently subscribed list. FTC requirements differ for transactional and commercial email; if marketing is later introduced, review consent/provider rules and commercial-email requirements separately. [FTC CAN-SPAM guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

### RISK-07 — Accessibility and asset rights

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the engineering target, with manual keyboard/screen-reader/zoom checks as well as automated checks. The DOJ explains ADA responsibilities for covered public-facing businesses; the exact legal application must not be confused with the separate state/local-government web rule. [DOJ guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/), [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

Publish a usable accommodation/support contact; provide a non-camera ticket lookup and text equivalent for QR information. Check field errors, focus, dialogs, contrast, touch targets, motion and payment fallback. Do not buy an accessibility overlay or call an automated score a legal certification.

Inventory logos, photographs, fonts, package images and copied text. Document permission/license for Kappa Sigma, sorority, UTA or Movember marks actually used; remove unauthorized material and unsupported affiliations. Link to approved products without treating a screenshot as a license to republish retailer photography. Keep dependency license notices where required.

### RISK-08 — Hosting and card-data obligations

Vercel restricts Hobby to noncommercial personal use and lists payment collection/product sales as commercial examples, while excepting requests for donations. Paid event admission is not clearly that donation-only exception. Treat plan eligibility as unresolved: obtain an applicable determination or select a permitted plan/host before release. Do not silently purchase an upgrade or assume a payment link avoids the restriction. [Vercel fair-use policy](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines).

Keep card number/CVC entry entirely in Stripe's supported component. Stripe reduces PCI scope; it does not eliminate merchant responsibilities. The account owner handles any compliance attestation required by Stripe, with no personal-dashboard access from us. [Stripe security guide](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide).

## What AI-built sites commonly overlook: concrete engineering work

Use this as a risk checklist, not a claim that all AI-built sites have these bugs. GitHub cautions that generated code can be insecure and needs review/testing. OWASP's 2025 risks cover access control, configuration, dependencies, cryptography, injection, design, authentication, integrity, logging and exceptional conditions. [GitHub responsible-use guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/responsible-use/agents), [OWASP Top 10](https://top10.owasp.org/2025/).

- **Authorization beyond the UI:** audit current and legacy APIs, exports, ticket/order tokens, refunds and staff provisioning. Test direct HTTP access with wrong roles, disabled users, expired/revoked sessions and another buyer's identifiers. No universal/shared legacy admin secret bypass in production.
- **Secrets and deployment boundaries:** scan tracked files/history, built client assets, source maps and CI artifacts without printing secrets. Ensure private `.data` and environment files stay excluded. Test wrong Stripe account/mode and preview isolation. Rotate an exposed credential; preserve encrypted-ticket recovery when changing encryption keys.
- **Abuse resistance:** retain existing global/account/checkout limits; test oversized/chunked bodies before expensive parsing, checkout/resend bursts, concurrent wrong-password attempts and legitimate check-in contention. Validate trusted proxy/IP handling. Add edge protections only where needed; do not declare the existing global login limit absent. Avoid a single attacker locking out every scanner.
- **Browser and output security:** add narrowly scoped CSP with Stripe's documented origins; verify actual deployment headers, HTTPS, camera Permissions-Policy and private caching across errors too. Keep camera access for check-in. Exercise script/HTML and spreadsheet-formula injection in real browser/email/export output, not only schema acceptance.
- **Supply chain:** audit the lockfile, production dependency advisories and licenses; triage reachability/severity, pin trusted CI actions and keep least-privilege CI permissions. Existing action pins/read-only permissions are present. Do not run blind force-upgrades or suppress relevant advisories merely to get a green check.
- **Failure recovery:** retain verified-payment fulfillment, replay protection, durable retries, per-ticket refunds, reversals and recovery with sales paused. Add provider-permission loss, email suppression, maximum-order messages, timeout-after-acceptance and worker-stopped alert cases. Never accept a redirect as proof of payment.
- **Resource limits and operations:** measure the serialized Postgres store under realistic concurrent scanning/payment work, clean stale sessions/attempts, and record backup/restore recovery time. Track queue age, payment exceptions, latency, database/storage/email quotas and alerts without buyer data. Run stress/abuse tests locally in isolation; hosted security/load testing needs provider-permitted scope. A free tier is not an uptime guarantee.
- **Finish the public experience:** remove stale Eventbrite/basketball/cause copy on cutover, test dead links/404s/empty and error states, mobile layouts and social metadata, avoid indexing private or test pages, and give buyers an honest delayed-email/support path. Keep old paid buyers valid and sales cutoff handling explicit.
